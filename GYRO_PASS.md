# Gyro Pass

Start `npm start` in `D:\REPlus\gyro_poc`, open http://localhost:3210, scan the QR, and select FiveM / REPlus. Press Stream, allow motion access, choose Horizontal or Vertical, then Record pass once the live feed is visible. Recording seeks to the current clip's beginning, starts playback, and stops/saves at its end. Stop & save ends early. The original position path and FOV remain in control; the phone supplies absolute world orientation, with heading alignment and physical pitch/roll.

## Direct gyro export

Recording no longer starts marker conversion. Export through REPlus loads the newest valid saved take for each project/clip identity and freezes those samples for the render. No phone connection is needed during export. The take is applied after the ordinary camera path and before optional shake, autofocus, and DOF aperture translation. Only rotation is replaced: position/FOV stay with the authored path.

The track is evaluated at the actual non-dilated replay timestamp on every camera update. Walking seeks, sliding's advancing clock, timed DOF samples, repeated presents, and out-of-order shutter samples use the same stateless quaternion interpolation. Gyro movement therefore participates in shutter accumulation rather than being held for an entire output frame. Partial takes hold their first/last aim outside the captured interval. Export never uses live input or the gyro replay transport, and does not pause/seek on the gyro track's behalf. The renderer and DOF controller own time.

CSV files remain under `plugins\RockstarEditorPlus\GyroTakes\`, including project/clip scope, replay timestamps, recorded position, quaternion and FOV. Files with the optional `# vertical 0/1` comment and older horizontal files both load. Invalid/truncated/non-monotonic takes are rejected. The renderer logs the chosen file, scope, sample count, time range, and orientation. It refuses export while recording or converting markers, and refuses mixed horizontal/vertical recorded tracks in one project export. Keep one format throughout an exported project. Missing scope prevents a take being applied to another clip.

## Full-resolution vertical video

Vertical records a +90-degree roll around the camera's local forward axis, turning the image left 90 degrees during rendering. The saved quaternion already includes this roll; export applies it once, before shake and DOF. Clips without a gyro track in the same portrait render also get sideways camera framing. FFmpeg's final encode uses `transpose=clock` on the completed rendered images. No crop, scale, or second lossy encode is introduced: a 2560x1440 game backbuffer becomes an upright 1440x2560 video. Actual resolution comes from the game capture, not a hardcoded size. This rotation applies to REPlus video output; raw image sequences remain sideways, and the game's fallback exporter does not use this pipeline.

## Optional marker conversion and blending

Convert to markers (optional), or Rockstar Editor+ > Gyro Pass > Bake Gyro Take to Markers, retains the previous marker tool. This is not required for export. It supports world-space free cameras without attach/look-at targets, respects engine placement/capacity limits, and backs up original markers/settings in `GyroTakes/before-bake-*.txt`. Undo is session-local and refuses to overwrite later marker edits. The dense saved gyro track remains authoritative for rotation during REPlus export even if markers were converted or subsequently edited.

Rockstar Editor+ > Blend > Apply Blending to All copies marker blend mode, easing, spline path/rotation and marker-local spline settings. Shake/focus stay separate. Those controls affect the ordinary marker path; they do not smooth the dense gyro rotation track. Raw recordings stay unchanged. Playback/export builds a separate motion curve: repeated identical poses held for at most 100 ms are collapsed, while long stationary holds and the final endpoint are retained. A time-aware cubic quaternion curve gives continuous velocity between ordinary samples. Segments across gaps longer than 150 ms use slerp to avoid inventing curvature across dropouts. Quaternion signs are aligned to prevent spins; no moving-average filter is applied to the phone motion.

## Streaming and validation

The existing ReShade/IgcsConnector capture add-on writes a three-file JPEG ring under GyroStream. The loopback bridge sends completed frames to the paired browser room over authenticated WSS, up to 10 fps at JPEG quality 65. Slow clients drop preview frames instead of accumulating a server queue. Streaming suspends for export/DOF and reports capture failures. Record enables only after a fresh image decodes. Changing the phone output format is disabled during recording/conversion.

The user verified earlier live control/recording; the previous marker-based export failed in-game. The replacement passed the native build, saved-track parser/interpolation checks, and a harness executing the production gyro export branch with game/capture boundaries stubbed. The harness uses the preserved 599-sample portrait take and checks shuffled/repeated/advancing replay times, frozen file snapshots, no transport writes, unchanged position/FOV, the rotated DOF basis and unrecorded-clip portrait framing. Actual walking/sliding exports with the capture/DOF add-on still require in-game validation.
New recordings stamp fresh sensor updates rather than copying the last phone pose on every game frame. The phone sends every valid sensor event without the former extra 16 ms throttle; WebSocket backpressure still drops input rather than building a stale queue. Browser control updates are cached so unchanged button state does not rewrite the DOM every animation frame. Replay and export use the same motion curve; original CSVs are retained. The phone video preview is still capped at 10 fps, independently of sensor updates. Offline checks cover continuous velocity at uneven knots, short repeated holds, genuine pauses, hemisphere flips, raw-file preservation, and fresh-packet recording. Physical-phone cadence and perceived smoothness require in-game validation.
